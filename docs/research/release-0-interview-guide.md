# Release 0 — Staff Interview Guide

Duration: 20–25 minutes  
Participants: 6–10 volunteers across all four role groups  
Format: Individual sessions where practical

## Research objective

Learn whether employees understand and value the accomplishment-to-garden loop without feeling evaluated, compared, or given another responsibility.

This is concept research, not an employee assessment. Responses should not be attributed to individuals in management reporting.

## Participant mix

Aim for representation from:

- 2–3 Support Desk employees
- 1–2 Project Team employees
- 1–2 vCIO / Strategic Advisors
- 1–2 Service or Project Managers

Adjust to staff availability. Participation should be voluntary, and managers should not pressure direct reports to participate or observe their sessions.

## Opening script

> We are exploring an optional internal experience called Common Grove. This session is about the product, not your performance. The prototype uses fictional examples and is not connected to our business systems. I did not create the screen you are about to see, so candid criticism is useful. There are no right answers. Please describe what you think and feel as you explore it.

Ask before recording audio, video, or identifying notes. If the participant declines, take anonymous notes only.

## Session tasks

### 1. First impression — 2 minutes

Show the initial screen without explaining the product loop.

Ask:

- What do you think this is?
- What do you think happened while the employee was away?
- What would you do first?

Observe whether the participant notices the companion, garden changes, welcome-back story, or role selector.

### 2. Cause and effect — 5 minutes

Ask the participant to open each garden change and read its private explanation.

Ask:

- What caused the starflower to bloom?
- Does the explanation feel clear?
- Does it feel appreciative, evaluative, or neither?
- What information would you never want shown here?

Do not teach the contribution categories unless the participant remains confused after exploring.

### 3. Role fairness — 5 minutes

Ask the participant to select their role under **Prototype lens**, then inspect all three examples. Briefly show one other role.

Ask:

- Do these examples resemble meaningful contributions in your work?
- Could someone in your role progress as naturally as someone in another role?
- What important contribution from your role is missing?
- Does any example reward quantity instead of meaningful progress?

### 4. Meaningful choice — 4 minutes

Select **See what is waiting**. Ask the participant to compare the orchard and workshop paths.

Ask:

- Does this choice feel interesting or like another task?
- Is it clear that the choice does not expire?
- Would you want choices like this occasionally?
- What would make the choice feel more personal?

### 5. Return and social comfort — 4 minutes

Ask:

- Would you voluntarily return to this experience? When?
- How would you feel if coworkers could visit your garden but never see the underlying work events?
- What should be private by default?
- What would make the experience feel like monitoring?
- What would make it feel like a genuine reward?

## Closing questions

- What is the strongest part of the concept?
- What is the weakest or most uncomfortable part?
- If we removed one thing, what should it be?
- What did you expect to be able to do but could not?
- Is there anything important I did not ask?

## Synthesis rule

Summarize patterns across participants rather than reporting individual reactions. Separate findings by role only when necessary to diagnose fairness, and suppress a role-level breakdown when the group is so small that a participant would be identifiable.
