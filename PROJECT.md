# Common Grove — Product Charter

## Product thesis

Accomplishments from existing workplace systems quietly grow a living garden populated by an autonomous companion.

Common Grove is an ambient reward and visualization layer for internal employees. It is not a performance-management dashboard.

## Initial audience

An internal team of approximately 14 people across:

- Support Desk
- Project Team
- vCIO / Strategic Advisor
- Service and Project Management

## Product principles

1. The garden never becomes another responsibility.
2. Absence never causes harm or lost progress.
3. Completion produces visible change.
4. Creatures behave like individuals, not progress bars.
5. Every role must have equitable access to progression.
6. Social features encourage appreciation, not ranking.
7. Private work information never appears in another person's garden.
8. Occasional choices matter more than frequent clicking.
9. Every visual and character must be original intellectual property.

## Working assumptions

These are provisional and may be changed after validation:

- Web-first, stylized 2.5D presentation
- One companion per employee
- One personal garden per employee
- Automatic use of ordinary resources
- No decay, neglect, or absence penalties
- Simulated accomplishment events before real integrations
- Role-specific work maps to universal contribution categories
- No employee rankings, grades, or public reward totals

## Universal contribution categories

1. **Helped someone succeed** — useful support, guidance, coaching, or client assistance.
2. **Moved important work forward** — a meaningful milestone, resolution, delivery, or decision.
3. **Strengthened the team or system** — documentation, prevention, improvement, mentoring, or reusable knowledge.

These categories describe contribution without requiring every role to perform the same activities.

## Initial non-goals

- Production integrations
- Authentication or organization administration
- Real-time multiplayer
- Full 3D navigation
- Competitive systems or leaderboards
- Breeding, complex economies, hunger, sickness, or death
- Mandatory check-ins or expiring choices
- Employee evaluation through garden activity

## First question to answer

Do employees understand and care about a garden that visibly changes because of their accomplishments, without feeling monitored or burdened?
