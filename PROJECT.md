# Common Grove — Product Charter

## Product thesis

Accomplishments from existing workplace systems quietly grow a living garden populated by autonomous companions.

Common Grove is an ambient reward and visualization layer for internal employees. It is not a performance-management dashboard.

## Initial audience

An internal team of approximately 14 people across:

- Support Desk
- Project Team
- vCIO / Strategic Advisor
- Service and Project Management

## Product principles

1. The garden never becomes another responsibility.
2. Absence never causes harm or lost progress.
3. Completion produces visible change.
4. Creatures behave like individuals, not progress bars.
5. Every role must have equitable access to progression.
6. Social features encourage appreciation, not ranking.
7. Private work information never appears in another person's garden.
8. Occasional choices matter more than frequent clicking.
9. Every visual and character must be original intellectual property.

## Working assumptions

These are provisional and may be changed after validation:

- Web-first, original stylized 3D presentation with bounded first-person exploration
- Multiple individual companions living together in each employee's personal garden; Pip is the first implemented resident, not the intended population limit
- One personal garden per employee
- Automatic use of ordinary resources
- No decay, neglect, or absence penalties
- Simulated accomplishment events before real integrations
- Role-specific work maps to universal contribution categories
- No employee rankings, grades, or public reward totals

## Universal contribution categories

1. **Helped someone succeed** — useful support, guidance, coaching, or client assistance.
2. **Moved important work forward** — a meaningful milestone, resolution, delivery, or decision.
3. **Strengthened the team or system** — documentation, prevention, improvement, mentoring, or reusable knowledge.

These categories describe contribution without requiring every role to perform the same activities.

## Initial non-goals

- Production integrations
- Authentication or organization administration
- Real-time multiplayer
- Open-world expansion beyond the approved 40 × 40 metre garden
- Competitive systems or leaderboards
- Breeding, complex economies, hunger, sickness, or death
- Mandatory check-ins or expiring choices
- Employee evaluation through garden activity

## First question to answer

Do employees understand and care about a garden that visibly changes because of their accomplishments, without feeling monitored or burdened?

## Current prototype scope

Four authored returns demonstrate that question over an illustrative season. Three fictional contributions establish the garden; subsequent visits accumulate flowers, a lived-in reading pavilion, and an optional Lantern Orchard or Tinker Workshop. Pip can remember a completed pet, snack, or toy interaction during the current session. His warmth and activity preferences change, without requiring care.

This is a local simulation, not elapsed-time computation, a working integration, or persistent memory. Refresh and Restart journey clear it. Owner-directed polish continues before anyone is invited to use the prototype.

## Owner clarification — 2026-09-05

The intended experience includes multiple creatures sharing the same personal garden, inspired by the population and interaction aspect of Chao Garden. This replaces the earlier one-companion working assumption. The owner approved a first milestone of Pip and two additional residents, with gradual arrivals and a preview shortcut for testing all three. The design accounts for individual identities, behavior, interaction targeting and memories, plus shared-space and shared-object coordination. The local prototype now implements this shared community; its design is recorded in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-05-shared-companion-garden-design.md`. This does not authorize multiplayer, breeding, needs or absence penalties, backend work, or publication. Preserve the approved visual style and Pip's identity.

## Spirit learning milestone — 2026-09-06

The approved first practical project is implemented locally: the expanded nook supplies a making-and-growing book, spirits learn simple assembly and planting by reading or observing, and one optional bundle supplies a shared planter. Autonomous task reservations, grounded travel, small tool props and completed stage changes make the work visible. One resident can complete the whole project; a larger community can share work. Occasional watering and inspection add life without decay or care obligations.

Progress lasts for the current session across comparison and subsequent visits; refresh or Restart journey clears it. Real-navigation tests cover both prerequisite orders and solo/three-resident completion within 240 active simulated seconds, plus interruption and recovery. Final browser evidence and verification limits are recorded in STATUS.md. Additional recipes, football and spectator multiplayer are future directions, outside this project; no networking, persistent storage, real workplace integration or hosting has been added.
